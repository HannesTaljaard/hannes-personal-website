This composition is a Hommage à Francis Poulenc, and it is especially his choir piece *Chansons à Boire* which shaped my composition. This is certainly not serious music. My *Chansons à Boire* is an attempt to understand the world in a playful manner. Of course, games sometimes teach serious matters. Maybe this work will encourage reflection on the world we live in. The composition is meant to evoke 'cosmic laughter' – Benjamin Zander's term – that smile we feel when we realise that our intense efforts are standing between us and our moments of insight. "How fascinating!"

I've never been captivated by drunkenness, mine or anybody else's. So, this work is not an 'Ode to being Drunk'. It presents a bunch of wits. Some of the wits are nitwits. Some say more, even in their inebriated state. The second movement wants to tell important stories.

The present version of the work has four movements and it was written in one university town, Potchefstroom, for another: Grahamstown. A short article that I wrote about this composition can be downloaded <a href="/assets/Chansons-a-Boire_COMMENTARY.pdf" target="_blank">here</a>.

1. Préambule (c. 3’10”)
2. Chorale (c. 9’45”)
3. Complainte (c. 1’30”)
4. Cadence (c. 5’00”)

Total duration c. 20’30”

Performers and listeners who acquaint themselves with the various compositions that formed the source material for my Chansons à Boire will likely draw more pleasure from the composition than those who approach it unarmed.

#### Préambule
This movement is based on Francis Poulenc’s *Chansons à Boire*, a song for unaccompanied male voices. I made a mash-up – changing the order of material, the registers, and so on… even the harmonies.

#### Chorale
If the Préambule is a mash up, then this movement is a potpourri of parts of Poulenc’s composition mixed with improvisations on folk songs from Auvergne. If one can imagine the stories that are told in the folk songs and the many characters who are telling their stories, this might be thought of as an opera without singers. Maybe a scenic version of this movement will be presented one day. Who knows…

#### Complainte
This movement presents one of Poulenc’s melodies. One can think of it as a reflection on, or reverberation of the previous (quasi-operatic) movement.

#### Cadence
The Cadence is an assembly of parts of two pieces by Poulenc. You could imagine that this music pokes fun in several directions. I never knew Poulenc, but I imagine that he did the same.
