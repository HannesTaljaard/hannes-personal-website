coloratura soprano and solo violin

These three compositions are recompositions of pieces that are very well-known to all singers and teachers of singing. I dedicated this composition to the singer Erica Eloff, and one of my aims was to compose a collection of pieces that would showcase her considerable vocal technique and talents as musician.

1. Bist Du Du c. 5’30”
2. Amare Illi c. 6’30”
3. Spevi Persugi c. 7’30”

Total duration c. 29 minutes

1 Bist Du Du
If you are with me, I will go peacefully. Let it be your beautiful hands that close my faithful eyes.
The text of this well-known song is in German, and the lyrics of the composition is a transformation of the original song text. I think of the composition as an intensification of the original, or as a more realistic depiction of the inner world of the persona, and of imagined interactions between people.

2 Amare Illi
Love that which is. Do not believe, do not doubt. Open your heart and see.
My beloved, the desire of my heart… You, my beloved.
The text of the original composition Amarilli mia bella, an Aria Antica by Giulio Caccini, is taken apart, and groups of words are rearranged. This uncommon way of ‘operating on the text’ creates a meditation on the nature of love and our way of enacting social realities, a meaning that searches deeper than the original text. The text can still be understood and is (more or less) in Italian. The virtuoso part for violin supports the virtuoso meditations of the soprano.
Giulio Caccini (1551-1618) wrote the song Amarilli mia bella, as part of a collection called Le nuove Musiche (1601), a collection that showcased his transformation of the familiar ‘musical language’ of his time. For the more than 420 years since its publication, the song Amarilli mia bella has been performed continuously. The song is in the repertoire of many singers all over the world, because it is a widely used didactic piece. Amare illi was composed as a transformation of Caccini’s well-known song, and as an experiment of how meaningful interaction with audience members can be construed through their familiarity with the source material. The following image can illuminate the process of recomposing Caccini’s song.
A beautiful ceramic vase (Caccini’s song) is dropped on the floor. The pieces are rearranged so that new, more abstract patterns arise: Caccini’s melodic material and text is combined differently to highlight other potential meanings. 
The fragments of the vase are now connected with gold (the sounds of the violin in the new composition) – creating an artwork as if through the Japanese technique of kintsugi. 
The gold creates a precious web that catches the observer: the familiarity of the music, combined with its enstrangement create an esthesic process that has the potential to draw the observer into co-creating the meaning of the new composition.


3 Spevi Persugi ssobra Ooco
For his pleasure Cupid mixes things up. Some of his victims faint, some fall.
This composition also takes the text and melody of an Aria Antica as the quarry from which its building blocks are taken: Spesso vibra per suo gioco by Alessandro Scarlatti. In this case the operation on the text is even more radical than the one that generated the text for Amare Illi: now the syllables (and not the words) are reordered to form the new text which sounds Italian, but which makes no sense. It is as if the cupid in Spevi persugi ssobra Ooco is even more mischievous (maybe evil) than the one in Scarlatti’s version.
