About four students and six lecturers were gathered at the university for a general discussion about neurodivergence. Hellen, dressed in black, was sitting at one of the ends of the semi-circle of chairs with headphones around her neck. After about half an hour of general ideas floating without any particular weight through the semi-circle, the group started to share experiences. Hellen mentioned that her noise-cancelling headphones are an essential protection when she is in the noise of the university buildings, and added some detail to ensure that other would understand what she meant, and that her intention is to help others. Everybody can benefit from this tool, she might have been thinking.<br><br>

Some of those attending looked with curiosity at the headphones – one had the idea that maybe they wanted to see which brand she has. Maybe they wanted to know if it is the same headphones that are waiting for them at home. Others looked at her incredulously, and one would not be amiss when one noticed a slight hint of impatience in the way that the turn only part of their bodies towards her, reluctantly. One of the incredulous ones questioned Hellen’s experience: the campus is certainly not so noisy that one needs noise-cancelling… it is, after all, a music university, and she is, after all, a musician. The eyes of the curious ones changed.<br><br>

These remarks of the leader of the pack of incredulous ones ambushed Hellen into sharing more personal experiences. Hellen maybe felt that she needs to convince the group that sensory sensitivities can indeed be very strong and that she was not making things up. She mentioned that, for example, she has to use a dry, powdered soap, because the change of state from dry to wet is unsupportable. The feeling in the room switched. With an uncanny simultaneity, a few people suddenly seemed to display the glee of children visiting the zoo and learning for the first time that the actual specimen of platypus that they are now seeing, is indeed a mammal. Other straightened their backs, tilted heads sideways towards sternums, and looked, more than listened, to the questions that erupted.<br><br>

When the questions subsided, when the questions lost their momentum and turned into speculations because of the lack of answers, the pack leader presented her experiences of many years of talking to ‘those people’. With the certainty of a therapist who knew exactly what her clients needed, she announced that whenever she talked to ‘one of those’ she always felt that, “there is something fundamentally wrong with this person”.<br><br>

Hellen turned her right shoulder ever so slightly inwards, her gaze trying to find some safety in the suddenly more tense knuckles of her left hand.<br><br>

You, dear reader, would be imagining the actual event, if you are now seeing some eyes growing softer, still looking at Hellen, and then sinking – emptier now – to rest on the floor.
